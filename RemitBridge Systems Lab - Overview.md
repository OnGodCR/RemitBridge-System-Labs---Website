

**RemitBridge Systems Lab**

*A Student-Led Applied Research and Community Access Initiative for Equitable Cross-Border Payments*

**Engineering faster, fairer ways for families to send money home.**

**Organization Development Proposal**

Proposed Founder and Executive Director: Angad

Eastlake High School | Sammamish, Washington

RemitBridge Systems Lab is a proposed student-led applied research and community-access initiative founded on Angad's research into international remittance systems. Its central academic question is which sharding, sidechain, or off-chain transaction architectures can most effectively increase throughput and reduce settlement cost for blockchain-based remittances, and how those systems could interoperate with existing fiat payment rails such as SWIFT-connected institutions and real-time gross settlement systems.

The organization would convert Angad's technical work into tangible community benefit through open-source benchmarking, an educational true-cost calculator, multilingual community workshops, student research fellowships, and public research reports. Its primary beneficiaries would be immigrant and refugee families who send or receive money across borders, along with students and researchers seeking accessible resources on payment-system engineering.

# **1\. Academic and Research Foundation**

## **Core Research Question**

*"What sharding, sidechain, or off-chain transaction architectures would most effectively increase throughput and reduce settlement cost for blockchain-based remittance systems, and how would interoperability between these architectures and existing fiat rails, such as SWIFT and RTGS, be achieved?"*

## **Problem Definition**

International remittances are transfers made by migrants and other workers to relatives or households in another country. These payments frequently support food, housing, education, medical care, emergencies, savings, and small-business activity. The social value of the transfer can therefore be much greater than its face value.

The current system can involve messaging networks, correspondent banks, foreign-exchange conversion, settlement institutions, compliance review, local distribution networks, and cash-pickup or digital-wallet providers. Each additional institution or conversion can add cost, delay, opacity, or operational risk. The advertised transfer fee may also fail to reveal the complete cost because exchange-rate markups and recipient-side charges can reduce the final amount received.

Blockchain-based payment infrastructure could theoretically reduce some intermediaries and accelerate settlement. However, base-layer networks may not be designed for the high-frequency, low-value traffic characteristic of global remittances. Sharding, sidechains, and off-chain payment channels address scaling differently, and none should be treated as automatically superior without a reproducible comparison that also accounts for security, liquidity, finality, energy use, regulation, and fiat interoperability.

## **Mission**

RemitBridge Systems Lab conducts student-led research on scalable and interoperable cross-border payment systems and translates that research into open-source benchmarks, accessible educational tools, multilingual community resources, and workshops that help immigrant and refugee families better understand remittance costs, exchange-rate markups, transfer delays, digital-payment risks, and emerging payment technologies.

## **Vision**

A world in which families do not lose urgently needed money or time because cross-border payment systems are expensive, fragmented, opaque, or inaccessible.

## **Purpose**

1. **Infrastructure failure:** Cross-border payments may depend on multiple institutions, inconsistent standards, settlement delays, limited interoperability, and complex compliance processes.

2. **Information failure:** Families may see advertised fees without clearly seeing exchange-rate markups, recipient-side charges, delivery conditions, or the complete amount the recipient will receive.

3. **Access failure:** The people most affected may have limited access to technical, financial, or multilingual explanations of how payment systems work and how transfer quotations can be evaluated safely.

# **3\. Theory of Change**

RemitBridge is designed around a direct research-to-impact pathway:

4. Angad conducts rigorous research on remittance infrastructure and blockchain scaling architectures.

5. The research produces a reproducible benchmark, datasets, code, and accessible explanations.

6. Those findings are translated into a true-cost calculator, multilingual guides, and workshops.

7. Community members gain a clearer understanding of costs, delays, risks, and available questions to ask before sending money.

8. Workshop data and community questions reveal new research needs, allowing RemitBridge to improve its tools and studies.

9. Student fellows are trained to continue the research, education, translation, and evaluation work, producing sustained leadership rather than a one-time project.

# **5\. Flagship Programs**

## **Program 1: RemitBench Open Testbed**

RemitBench would be an open-source experimental environment that models realistic remittance workloads and compares sharded architectures, sidechains, off-chain payment channels, and a conventional baseline. It would publish the code, assumptions, synthetic data, test conditions, and limitations needed for another student or researcher to reproduce the results.

Core measurements would include:

15. Transactions processed per second under ordinary and peak loads.

16. Average latency, tail latency, settlement time, and finality time.

17. Estimated transaction, bridge, liquidity, and conversion costs.

18. Failure recovery, validator or node disruption, and bridge-risk assumptions.

19. Channel capacity, routing, and rebalancing requirements.

20. Energy consumption per transaction under equivalent workloads.

21. Interoperability complexity with fiat institutions, APIs, and payment-message standards.

## **Program 2: TrueCost Remittance Calculator**

The TrueCost Remittance Calculator would be a free educational tool that calculates the effective cost of a transfer and the amount received after considering the amount sent, advertised fee, quoted exchange rate, benchmark exchange rate, recipient-side charge, and estimated delivery time. It would explain every calculation and identify which information is missing from a quotation.

The tool would not move money, connect to wallets, request immigration information, collect banking credentials, recommend investments, or promise that a particular provider is always best.

## **Program 3: Money Across Borders Workshops**

RemitBridge volunteers would deliver partner-reviewed workshops that explain how remittances work, how to calculate the complete transfer cost, why exchange-rate markups matter, how delivery differs from final settlement, how blockchain and payment channels work, and how to recognize common remittance or cryptocurrency scams. Materials would be translated only with qualified language and community review.

## **Program 4: RemitBridge Student Research Fellows**

Students would join one of four working teams and complete defined outputs rather than holding nominal ambassador titles:

22. Systems Research Team: benchmark design, simulations, code, and technical literature.

23. Economic Analysis Team: fees, exchange rates, provider structures, corridor data, and market design.

24. Community Education Team: workshops, accessible explainers, partner communication, and public presentations.

25. Language Access and Evaluation Team: translation coordination, cultural review, participant feedback, and impact measurement.

## **Program 5: Cross-Border Payment Access Brief**

Once each year, RemitBridge would publish a concise report presenting benchmark results, community questions, recurring sources of consumer confusion, calculator findings, policy and interoperability recommendations, research limitations, and the following year's research agenda.

# **11\. Academic and Admissions Distinction**

RemitBridge would be strongest when it demonstrates a coherent progression from advanced academic interest to tangible public benefit. The organization should not rely on the title 'founder' or on nonprofit incorporation as evidence of achievement. Its distinction would come from original research, a functioning product, responsible community implementation, credible review, sustained leadership, measurable learning, and public documentation of both results and limitations.

This structure reflects recurring characteristics visible among successful Coca-Cola Scholars: students connect a serious academic field to a clearly defined population, produce concrete resources or services, work through credible institutions, lead over time, and report measurable outcomes. For Angad, the distinctive combination is payment-systems research, open-source engineering, international finance, immigrant-community education, and evidence-based evaluation.
